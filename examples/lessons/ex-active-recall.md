Re-reading notes feels productive, but it mostly builds *familiarity*. **Active recall** means closing
the book and pulling the idea out of your own memory. That effort is what makes it stick.

## How to do it
1. Read a short section (one page, one video chapter).
2. Close it. Write down, from memory, the 3 main points.
3. Open it again and check. Fix what you missed in a different color.

| Habit | Feels | Works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Re-reading | easy | weakly |
| Highlighting | busy | weakly |
| Self-testing | hard | **well** |

## Try it
Pick anything you studied this week and do the three steps above. It takes five minutes.

```quiz
question: Which of these is **active recall**?
A: Reading your notes a second time
B: Highlighting the key sentences
C: Writing the main points from memory, then checking
D: Watching the lecture at 2x speed
answer: C
explain: Only C makes you retrieve the idea yourself. Re-reading (A) is the tempting one, but it only builds familiarity.
```

```quiz
question: Why does self-testing feel harder than re-reading?
A: Because it is less effective
B: Because retrieving takes effort, and that effort strengthens memory
C: Because you need special software
answer: B
explain: The difficulty is the point: effortful retrieval is what makes memories last.
```
